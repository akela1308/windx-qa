How the first call goes

I do not know where to start. What do I say?

Tell us what has happened in your own words. We will ask the questions and write down what matters.

Will you tell me what it is going to cost?

Yes, before you decide anything. Where the work can be priced as a fixed fee, we give you the figure in writing.

What if I only want to understand my options?

That is a perfectly good reason to ring. Twenty minutes is enough to lay out the choices and what each would involve.

What happens after the call?

If you want us to act, we send a short letter setting out the steps and the fee. If you do not, you owe us nothing.

Questions people ask us

Do I need to bring documents to the first call?

Not for the call itself. If you have a marriage certificate, a will or a letter from a bank to hand, mention it and we will say what to gather afterwards.

Can I come to the office, or is it phone only?

Either. The call can be by phone, and if you would rather sit down with us, we will arrange a time at the office.

Do you act for both people in a divorce?

No. We act for one of you only, so that the advice you get is yours alone.

How long does probate take?

It depends on the estate and on how quickly the banks and the court respond. On the call we will give you an honest range for your case.

Two partners and a paralegal

Hartwell & Okafor is Clare Hartwell and Tunde Okafor, with a paralegal who keeps the files moving. We are a small practice in Islington, so the person you speak to on the first call is the person who does your work.

Most people come to us at a difficult moment: a marriage ending, a parent who has died, a will that has been put off for years. Many are worried about what it will cost. That is why we use fixed fees wherever the work allows, and why the first call is free.

We write to you in ordinary English, we tell you the next step after every conversation, and we do not start work until you have seen the fee.

You should know the price before you decide anything.
