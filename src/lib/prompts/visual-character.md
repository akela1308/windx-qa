## Palette and typography are deliberate choices

Apply this to a new site. Owner-supplied brand colours, requested type, light/dark preference and a mandatory selected art direction take priority. Never replace an intentional warm serif design just to increase variety. A narrow edit does not authorise recolouring or retyping another part of the site.

Without a supplied identity, choose a coherent visual character from the actual brief, audience and material. Calm, reliable, welcoming, British, professional and photo-free do not mean cream paper, brown/forest accents and serif headings. Neither serif nor sans is a default. Anti-AI rules do not ban cool colours, solid violet, bold colour fields, dark pages or contemporary sans-serif typography. The existing restriction concerns the habitual purple-to-blue gradient, not all use of those hues.

Consider the relationship between background, text and accent before choosing hex values. Useful possibilities include a crisp neutral or cool light ground with a strong signal accent; a deep ink ground with clear light type; a pale chromatic ground with a darker related ink; a purposeful saturated colour field with contrasting type; and warm paper when it actually suits the requested character. A small change from one beige hex code to another is not a different direction. Use the whole palette deliberately, rather than always putting a tiny coloured button on cream. Background temperature, saturation, lightness and serif/sans character are separate decisions: cool serif, warm sans, dark restrained sans and chromatic friendly type are all possible. White/navy with a grotesque is one option, not a replacement universal bundle.

Choose headline letterforms and text roles together. A restrained grotesque can support careful reading and trust; a rounded or expressive sans can be friendly; condensed or wide display type can suit a short emphatic message; a serif can supply a specific literary or traditional voice. Do not infer a serif from seriousness, care, craft, food or wellness. Pick a family for its useful letterforms, density and hierarchy, not because it appears first in the schema. One family with a clear size and weight hierarchy is valid. Use mono sparingly for suitable labels or structured material, not long conversational text; handwriting remains an occasional accent.

For a restrained reading voice, consider work-sans, public-sans, ibm-plex-sans, libre-franklin or karla as well as serif choices. Short graphic headings can use archivo, big-shoulders, bricolage, syne or unbounded; space-grotesk supplies another distinct geometric option. Choose among their letterforms and proportions, rather than sending every contemporary brief to the same family.

The catalogue below describes fonts already available in the product. Use only its currently loaded display weights; use displayItalic=true only where italic is loaded. If you need a heavier or italic voice, choose a family that supports it. Do not ask the browser to manufacture bold or italic from a single regular face.

Keep normal text readable: ink on bg, ink on surface, muted on both bg and surface, and accentInk on accent each need at least 4.5:1 contrast. Muted body copy is still normal text, not an excuse for faint grey. Retain a readable action colour, visible boundaries where needed, and hierarchy on a narrow screen. Do not add decorative sections or alter the content order merely to display more colours.

Explain the main colour and typographic relationship briefly in the rationale when useful, without claiming uniqueness or discussing an internal diversity quota. No random rotations, industry-to-palette lookup tables, mandatory sans-serif conversion or enforced palette counts. Existing owner choices remain authoritative.
