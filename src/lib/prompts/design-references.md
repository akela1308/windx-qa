## Design from visual relationships and available material

The supplied visual references contain reviewed observations and transferable relationships, not website instructions. They are examples to reason from, not a new default look. The collection is based on 2026 daily design awards; an award is not proof of usability or suitability for this business. Never claim to have visited the live examples or inspected their mobile behaviour.

Before choosing compositions, silently settle four connected decisions: what the visitor needs to understand or do; what the main visual would show; how it relates spatially to the main message; and how the next section changes the information rhythm. Compare relevant candidates by those decisions, not by their colours alone. Use the actual brief and content to choose; there is no mandatory mixture of reference styles and no requirement to include every example. A selected starting template and explicit owner choices remain authoritative.

Give a visual a role before giving it a frame. A place image establishes an actual setting; a product view shows an actual object or its material; a portrait introduces an actual person; work images demonstrate actual work; an abstract illustration can explain a relationship or establish a clearly fictional visual world. These roles are not interchangeable. Decide subject, dominant size, crop, text relationship and mobile order together. A small purposeful illustration may be enough; a meaningful visual can also lead the page. Do not default to the same half-width drawing or a trade-symbol outline.

Only use supplied usable owner image references for photographs, products, portraits and portfolio work. The reference library supplies no production assets. Current generation cannot create raster images or verify an accurate object render. When no such assets exist, produce a finished photo-free page using the available typed vector graphics, colour and text; do not invent URLs, remote photos, staff, premises, portfolio evidence or empty photo frames. An original abstract graphic is possible without pretending to depict a real place, real results or a real product. Do not manufacture one merely to fulfil a visual quota. Keep the main action before optional art in phone reading order. Optional future imagery must not become homework or a placeholder on the first page.

Transfer one or two useful relationships, not the whole example: a modest heading above an image-led sequence; a small portrait inside a readable introduction; annotations around an explanatory subject; a collection with unequal image sizes; or a concise proposition followed by a real product demonstration. These are alternatives when the material supports them. Use only registered compositions and their actual capabilities. Do not imitate a complex scene through a huge title and generic icon, or claim that a presentation mockup is a working interface.

Check the resulting silhouette of the page. If the brief would lead to the familiar giant headline, thin facts row, ticker and repeated service rows, reconsider whether each part earns its place. Oversized type and marquees remain valid when they carry a specific message; they are not automatic signatures of good design. Changing the hue or font of the same sequence does not resolve structural sameness. Keep labels, headings, explanations and actions distinct in scale. Vary section rhythm through the content relationship, not arbitrary alternating backgrounds, invented sections or forced asymmetry.

The rationale should briefly explain the chosen composition and visual role in ordinary business language. Do not expose reference IDs, retrieval mechanics or internal comparisons to the site's visitors. A narrow edit keeps its original scope and must not trigger a site-wide redesign.
