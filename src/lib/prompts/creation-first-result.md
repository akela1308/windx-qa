## First result: a finished proposal using the information already available

The first result must be a usable designed page, not a list of homework for its owner. Honour the requested visual character, brand colours and exclusions before trade stereotypes. British English and the target market do not prescribe newspaper typography, ledgers, ruled columns or editorial layouts.

No supplied photographs is a normal starting condition. Use photo-free compositions, deliberate typography, colour, shapes or a relevant abstract vector illustration. Avoid compositions with required photo slots when there is no usable owner image. Do not fill the page with descriptions of pictures the owner should take, upload instructions, grey photo frames or requests for missing materials. Do not invent image URLs, photographs of real premises, staff portraits, testimonials, certifications or an approved logo. A typographic business name can stand on its own; an original abstract graphic is not evidence about the real business.

Use owner assets if available; their absence must not delay the first result. Do not ask for a batch of photos, a logo, a brand book or documents in the rationale or site copy. Optional enrichment happens later when the owner asks for it. Keep unknown business facts in factsToConfirm rather than turning them into an initial questionnaire or invented certainty. Existing publication confirmation remains necessary.

Write the rationale as a short explanation of the finished result, not instructions to supply materials. The output is still the required site document, not a plan. Do not apply these first-result rules to remove photos from an existing site during a narrow edit.

## Composition follows content, not a default skeleton

Before writing the document, silently decide what the visitor needs first, the position of the main message, the relationship between text and visuals, and the next useful section. This is internal design work, not another question or an extra AI call. Changing colours, fonts or tones alone is not a different layout.

There is no default hero. Start from the visitor's main verb: check, choose, read, browse or see. The mere presence of a price, time or address does not decide the layout. Consider the hierarchy of the whole brief and the requested character:
- A short direct proposition with a few facts can use hero.type-poster: the statement leads and the facts form a compact line below. This can suit an energetic, practical message without needing either a picture or a side panel.
- Use hero.editorial when a principal offer has several conditions that really need a separate compact reference panel. The photo is optional. Do not turn every priced service or every set of opening hours into this same arrangement; never invent facts to fill the panel.
- For browsing distinct groups or choosing different next steps, hero.action-desk can put real short routes beside the explanation. Link to the relevant sections, not a set of identical dead-end actions. Detailed content belongs below rather than being repeated in full at the top.
- When understanding the proposition is the main task, hero.statement gives it a short readable heading and a separate explanation, without a competing picture. A dictionary definition or disclaimer is not the h1. Long service explanations can follow in prose or disclosures.
- When the result of the work or the collection is itself visual, hero.center-stage can put a meaningful original illustration below the message and action. Known practical details can sit in the supporting text; they do not automatically outrank the visual subject.
- Use hero.split-graphic when a drawing actually explains a relationship or process better beside the text. An outline cup, chair, leaf, tool or other generic trade symbol alone does not justify a half-width illustration. If removing the drawing loses no useful explanation, choose another opening and place optional art later.
These are content relationships, not fixed industry templates, rotation slots or quotas. Do not prefer a composition because it was listed first or is newer. Explicit owner composition requests take priority. Use supplied usable photos when they matter; do not invent them.

Choose the next sections from actual visitor questions and the content hierarchy. A collection can follow the opening immediately; practical services can lead into contact; an explanation can precede a detailed service list. services.staggered, services.disclosure, prose-columns and feature-plus-list present different relationships. Do not automatically use hero.type-poster + services.index + marquee + process + proof + FAQ on every page. Do not repeat the same information in both a hero action list and the next section just to fill space. Fewer useful sections are better than invented business claims. Repeating the same price or message as a facts panel, a giant figure and an owner note adds no new visitor task; choose the presentation that does the job. Repetition is useful for an actual comparison or a relevant next action, not to decorate another section.

For the new hero graphics, draw only the typed paths allowed by the schema in the 400 by 300 coordinate space. No raw SVG, no external images and no requests to upload assets. Keep the main action before the graphic in the phone reading order. A graphic is optional as a design decision: use a text/task composition when a drawing would add nothing. Do not add irrelevant art to meet a diversity quota.
